For Schools & Districts

Bring Wholeness Education to your institution.

A clear path for principals, superintendents, and district leaders to bring the Wholeness Operating System to your teachers, your students, and the families you serve.

Start the conversation

Where most schools begin

Teaching for Wholeness™, built for institutional scale.

Most schools and districts begin with Teaching for Wholeness™. Your teachers move through certification individually, using the institutional email your school or district issues them, which ties each teacher's progress to an official record from day one. From there, many schools go on to bring Learning for Wholeness™ to their students and point interested families toward Parenting for Wholeness™, though every pathway remains complete entirely on its own, and nothing requires the others to begin.

How it works

From first conversation to a working partnership.

1

A conversation

We talk through your school or district's own starting point, so the right first step is mapped out together, not assumed.

2

Teacher certification

Your teachers begin Teaching for Wholeness™ using their institutional email, carrying their progress through four levels of certification.

3

Extending the work

When your school is ready, Learning for Wholeness™ opens to students and Parenting for Wholeness™ to families, each on its own timeline.

4

An ongoing partnership

Growth here is continuous, not a single rollout, and we stay in the conversation as the work deepens across your community.

What your community gains

One architecture, carried by everyone around a student.

Teachers

A certification that deepens over four levels

From Wholeness Foundations Teacher through Wholeness Ecosystem Leader, each level building on the one before it.

Students

A four-year developmental pathway

Learning for Wholeness™ moves students through the same twelve-intelligence architecture their teachers are learning to carry.

Families

A parenting pathway that mirrors the work

Parenting for Wholeness™ gives families a way to grow alongside their children, instead of being asked to catch up to them.
