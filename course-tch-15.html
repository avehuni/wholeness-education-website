About this module

Thinking about thinking can be taught, not just hoped for.

Metacognition, the ability to notice and direct one's own thinking, is usually treated as a byproduct that some students happen to develop and others simply don't, rather than as a skill that can be taught as directly as any academic content. This module reimagines cognitive intelligence along those lines, giving a teacher concrete ways to make thinking visible and teachable rather than leaving it to develop by accident.

What you'll explore

Three shifts this module is building toward.

  • Why metacognition functions as a teachable skill rather than a fixed trait.
  • A few concrete ways to make a student's own thinking process visible to them.
  • What changes in deep learning once metacognition is taught directly.

Three lanes of knowing

Understood, practiced, and explored.

Lane 1 · Science

Metacognition as a skill

What's understood about how directly teaching metacognitive strategies improves deep learning and transfer, more than content coverage alone.

Lane 2 · Practice

A thinking-aloud practice

A short practice of modeling thinking aloud for students, making an invisible cognitive process visible and learnable.

Lane 3 · Meaning-making

How was my own thinking taught to me?

Reflection on whether this teacher's own metacognitive skill was taught deliberately or left to develop on its own.

Outcome

"I actively teach metacognition now, rather than hoping it develops on its own."
