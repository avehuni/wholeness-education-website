About this module

Holding a limit without losing the relationship.

Teenagers often treat boundaries and friendship as being in tension with each other, as if saying no to a friend puts the whole relationship at risk. This module offers a different model: a boundary stated clearly and kindly is usually what keeps a friendship workable over time, not what threatens it. Working through real, specific friend scenarios, it builds the practical language for holding a limit, staying warm while doing it, and trusting that a friendship strong enough to matter is also strong enough to hold a little honesty.

What they'll explore

Four shifts this module is building toward.

  • Why a clearly stated boundary tends to strengthen a friendship rather than damage it.
  • A few specific, kind ways of saying no to a friend without over-explaining or apologizing excessively.
  • How to tell the difference between a friendship that can hold a boundary and one that can't.
  • Practice naming one boundary they've been avoiding with a specific friend.

Three lanes of knowing

Understood, practiced, and explored.

Lane 1 · Science

What boundaries actually protect

A look at boundary-setting research in adolescent friendships, and why clear limits predict longer-lasting closeness.

Lane 2 · Practice

Saying it kindly and clearly

Practice with specific phrasing for holding a boundary with a friend without over-apologizing for it.

Lane 3 · Meaning-making

Can this friendship hold my honesty?

Reflection on a specific friendship and a boundary that's been quietly overdue.

Outcome

"I can hold a boundary with a friend and trust the friendship to hold it too."
